# Independent verification of `ep.r71-3.grant-communication` v1

Date: 2026-11-03. Base: `claude/r0-deadline-tracer` at `2b92bef` (PR #1). Scope: the rule v1, `src/date.ts`, `src/calendars.ts`, `src/calendars/epo-2025.ts`, `src/calendars/epo-2026.ts` and `cases/ep.r71-3.grant-communication.v1.json`.

The reviewer did not write the code. Each fact below was checked against the primary source on epo.org, which was downloaded on 2026-11-03. The code, comments, README and golden cases were not used as evidence.

This review does not set `reviewedBy`. Only a patent attorney may set it.

## Summary

- The engine calculates the correct due date for every document date that was checked: 1,308 document dates from 2023-09-01 to 2027-03-31 (722 due dates, 525 `calendar-missing` refusals, 61 `rule-not-effective` refusals), and the closure roll for each of the 730 days of 2025 and 2026. The oracle that checked them uses integer arithmetic and its own copy of the closing days. It does not use code from `src/`.
- The 2025 and 2026 calendars agree with OJ EPO 2024, A91 and OJ EPO 2025, A40 on every day and every filing office.
- All 13 original golden cases are correct.
- 5 items were WRONG. None of them changed a due date for valid input. Each has a fix and a test in this branch.
- The original tests did not catch 4 of 25 likely bugs. The new tests catch all 25 (`node scripts/mutation-check.mjs`).
- In 15 real EP applications, the register data agrees with the engine. In one application (EP4547754), the register records the loss of rights as effective on the day after the date that the engine calculates. This is the expected result.

## 4. Gaps and new tests

### New golden cases (26)

Each date was calculated by hand before the test ran. All are in `cases/ep.r71-3.grant-communication.v1.json`.

- Document date on a closed day: 2025-05-29 (Ascension Day) gives 2025-09-29. 2025-12-25 gives 2026-04-25 (Saturday), then Sunday, then King's Day, so 2026-04-28.
- Runs of closed days: Easter 2025 (2024-12-18 gives 2025-04-22) and Easter 2026 (2025-12-03 gives 2026-04-07).
- Days closed at one or two offices: 2025-01-05 gives 2025-05-06; 2026-02-04 gives 2026-06-05; 2025-09-06 gives 2026-01-07; 2025-06-03 gives 2025-10-06.
- A 2025 closed day that ends a 2024 period: 2024-09-01 gives 2025-01-02.
- Month end: 29 October 2025 gives 2026-02-28, then 2026-03-02. Day 31 that exists: 2026-01-31 gives 2026-05-31 (Sunday), then 2026-06-01. Day 30: 2025-11-30 gives 2026-03-30; 2026-04-30 gives 2026-08-30 (Sunday), then 2026-08-31. 31 August 2025 gives 31 December 2025, then 2026-01-02, with both notices cited.
- The 7-day boundary: receipt after 8 days gives a note of 1 day. Receipt on the document date gives no note.
- Late receipt on a closed four-month date (L8): 2025-06-11, received 2025-06-23, gives the later date 2025-10-16.
- OJ EPO 2023, A29, example 1 moved to 2025: 2025-11-13, received 2025-11-25, gives 2026-03-13 and a later date of 2026-03-18.
- Receipt before the document date: no change and no note. The founder asked for a refusal here. This review does not agree. OJ EPO 2023, A29, section II says that an addressee can access a Mailbox document before its date, and the date of the document still decides. A refusal would block a valid input. The case pins the current behaviour.
- Years without a calendar: the first effective document date 2023-11-01 (2024 is not loaded), 2024-08-31, 2026-08-24 and 2026-08-28 (the closure rolls into 2027), 2027-11-15 (2028), and 2027-10-31 (29 February 2028). Each must give `calendar-missing`.
- An unknown office in the event code (`XYZ:R71(3)`) gives `unknown-event`.

### Bad input (WRONG, fixed)

| # | Input from a JavaScript caller that did not use the parse functions | Before | After |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | `documentDate: '2026-02-30'` | Silent due date 2026-06-30 | `invalid-input` |
| B2 | `receivedAt: '2025-03-99'`, `'yesterday'`, `''`, or no `receivedAt` field | Silent due date, and a late receipt was silently ignored | `invalid-input` |
| B3 | `documentDate: '2025-9-1'` or `'2025-03-03T00:00'` | Uncaught `RangeError: Invalid time value` | `invalid-input` |
| B4 | No `documentDate` field | Uncaught `TypeError` | `invalid-input` |
| B5 | `documentDate: ''` | `rule-not-effective` with an empty date | `invalid-input` |

Source: the founder's rule that bad input must give a clear refusal and never a silent date. The TypeScript types forbid these values, but the package is plain JavaScript at runtime, and `parseLocalDate` exists because callers can pass bad text.

Fix: `defineRule` now parses `event`, `documentDate` and `receivedAt` again before it computes, and returns `{ ok: false, error: 'invalid-input', detail }`. The detail names each bad field. `ComputationError` has the new value `invalid-input`. Test: "compute refuses input that a JavaScript caller did not parse" in `test/engine.test.mjs`.

### Exhaustive oracle

`test/oracle.test.mjs` holds a second implementation written from the legal text. It uses integer month arithmetic, a leap-year rule, Zeller's congruence for the weekday, and a per-office copy of each notice. It checks:

- the calendar files against the notices (every day of both years);
- `rollClosure` on every day of 2025 and 2026 (730 days, so every closed day and the day before and after it);
- `compute` for every document date from 2023-09-01 to 2027-03-31 (1,308 dates): the due date or the refusal, the plain four-month date in the trace, `add-period` or `month-end`, the start day, that the due date is never before the plain date, that the due date is never a closed day, and that the closure-roll step cites each calendar that it read;
- the 28th, 29th, 30th and 31st of every month of 2024 to 2026.

Result: 0 disagreements.
